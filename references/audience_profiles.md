# Audience profiles

Map the audience string to one profile by its dominant cues. When two
profiles fit, choose the one whose decision goal is stricter and note the
choice in `uncertainty`.

## 1. domain_expert — specialist conference / workshop session

- **Cues:** "conference session", "symposium", "workshop", named research
  area, peers in the same subfield.
- **Prior knowledge:** high. Shared terminology, standard methods and
  canonical baselines need no explanation.
- **Decision goal:** accept that the method/result is novel and sound
  relative to the field's current frontier.
- **Background ratio:** ≤10% of talk slides; open almost on the problem.
- **Evidence depth:** full mechanism, ablations, baselines, failure cases,
  exact metrics. Show the numbers, not rounded adjectives.
- **Claim strength:** precise and hedged to the measured scope; experts
  punish overreach. Explicitly position against named prior work.
- **Q&A pressure:** highest; mechanism and controls get the most backup
  slides.

## 2. mixed_seminar — cross-group research seminar / biomedical seminar

- **Cues:** "research seminar", "department seminar", "group meeting with
  guests", mixed specialties.
- **Prior knowledge:** strong scientists, uneven subfield knowledge.
- **Decision goal:** understand the question, approach and why the evidence
  matters; remember the takeaway.
- **Background ratio:** 15–25%. One slide defines the key concept and
  notation; acronyms expanded on first use.
- **Evidence depth:** lead with the biological/physical meaning of each
  figure; keep dense tables in backup, show key rows on the main deck.
- **Claim strength:** confident on the main finding, careful about
  translational or cross-domain extrapolation.
- **Q&A pressure:** broad; expect "what does this mean for X?" and
  mechanism-in-plain-language questions.

## 3. committee — defense / review / evaluation panel

- **Cues:** "defense", "thesis", "review panel", "evaluation", "committee",
  "grant review".
- **Prior knowledge:** expert but time-constrained; they judge framing and
  boundaries as much as content.
- **Decision goal:** endorse the candidate's contribution as original,
  independent and correctly bounded.
- **Background ratio:** 10–15%, framed as motivation, not a tutorial.
- **Evidence depth:** explicit contribution list, clear novelty vs prior
  work, and a candid limitations slide treated as a strength.
- **Claim strength:** every "first/novel" claim must be directly evidenced;
  scope statements are mandatory.
- **Q&A pressure:** adversarial but fair; prepare limitation, alternative
  explanation and "what if" backup pages.

## 4. collaborator — internal team / lab meeting / progress update

- **Cues:** "group meeting", "team update", "lab meeting", "progress",
  "collaborators".
- **Prior knowledge:** full project context; they care about status and
  risk.
- **Decision goal:** align on progress, unblock decisions, agree next
  steps.
- **Background ratio:** near zero; one reminder sentence at most.
- **Evidence depth:** interim results, pending analyses, what worked and
  what failed, concrete risks and timelines.
- **Claim strength:** provisional; label preliminary vs confirmed results.
- **Q&A pressure:** operational; prepare data-quality, workload and
  next-step slides.

## 5. general — interdisciplinary / lay / broad audience

- **Cues:** "general audience", "interdisciplinary", "public", "introductory
  talk", non-specialist venue.
- **Prior knowledge:** variable, assume no subfield training.
- **Decision goal:** convey why the question matters and the headline
  result, without technical exclusion.
- **Background ratio:** 25–35%; define every specialized term, use
  analogies and conceptual figures over raw tables.
- **Evidence depth:** one or two signature figures; statistics explained in
  plain language; full tables move to backup.
- **Claim strength:** measured; avoid field-specific superlatives the
  audience cannot evaluate.
- **Q&A pressure:** open-ended; prepare "why does this matter" and ethics /
  application answers.

## Adaptation rules that always apply

- Tight time (≤10 min) cuts background first, then secondary results —
  never the spine or the limitations.
- A small slide budget forces figure-first slides; a large budget allows
  mechanism build-up and comparison pages.
- The audience changes emphasis and ordering, never the facts: the same
  evidence must be presented with different depth, never replaced.
